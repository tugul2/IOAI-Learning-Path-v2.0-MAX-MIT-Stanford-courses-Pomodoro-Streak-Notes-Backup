Skill Checklist for Top 5%

Think of this as the finished product: by the end, you should be comfortable with all of the following.

∑

1.1 Mathematics

You do not need Olympiad math, but you must be fast and fluent with the math that powers ML/AI.

LA

Linear Algebra

  • Vectors, matrices, norms, dot product, matrix multiplication, transpose, inverse.
  • Eigenvalues/eigenvectors, diagonalization, spectral decomposition in the context of PCA and SVD.
  • Rank, null space, projections, orthogonality.
P

Probability & Statistics

  • Random variables, expectation, variance, covariance, independence.
  • Common distributions: Bernoulli, binomial, uniform, normal, etc.
  • Law of large numbers, central limit intuition (informal is fine).
  • Bayes' theorem, priors/posteriors, likelihood; naive Bayes classifier.
∇

Calculus & Optimization

  • Derivatives, partial derivatives, gradients, chain rule.
  • Gradient descent and variants (SGD, momentum, Adam). Explain step‑by‑step updates for simple models.
  • Convex vs non‑convex intuition; local minima/saddle points.
⚡ Minimum bar: you can derive a one‑step gradient update for a tiny neural network on paper and explain why that step makes sense.
Py

1.2 Programming & Environment Fluency

You should treat Python and the IOAI toolchain as "native language".

PY

Core Python

  • Control flow, functions, list/dict/set comprehension, classes.
  • Reading/writing files, argument parsing, basic debugging.
SCI

Scientific Python Stack

  • NumPy for vectorized operations, broadcasting, linear algebra.
  • Pandas for tabular data (joins, groupby, pivot, missing values, data cleaning).
  • Matplotlib/Seaborn or Plotly for quick exploratory data analysis.
ML

ML/DL Libraries

  • Scikit‑learn for classical ML (models, pipelines, CV, metrics).
  • PyTorch (or equivalent) for defining, training, and debugging neural networks (custom modules, training loops, optimizers).
ENV

Contest Environment

  • Comfortable coding in Jupyter on a standardized environment (like Bohrium) with limited internet and only whitelisted docs + integrated ChatGPT 4o.
  • No dependency on high‑level AutoML / hyperparameter frameworks or external APIs.
ML

1.3 Classical Machine Learning

You should understand these models conceptually, and be able to implement useful pipelines around them.

  • ›Supervised: linear regression, logistic regression, K‑NN, decision trees, random forests, gradient boosting, SVM.
  • ›Unsupervised: K‑means, hierarchical clustering, Gaussian mixture intuition, PCA, basic manifold methods (t‑SNE/UMAP conceptually).
  • ›Model evaluation: train/val/test splits, cross‑validation, metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC‑AUC, logloss).
  • ›Common patterns: bias–variance trade‑off, under/overfitting, regularization (L1/L2), data leakage.
DL

1.4 Deep Learning

Based on IOAI syllabus (neural networks, embeddings, attention, Transformers).

FND

Fundamentals

  • Perceptron, feed‑forward networks, activation functions.
  • Losses: MSE, cross‑entropy; probabilistic interpretation.
  • Backpropagation, gradients, mini‑batch training.
ARC

Architectures

  • MLPs for tabular and simple structured data.
  • CNNs for images (convolution, pooling, batch norm, residual connections).
  • Attention and Transformer architectures (self‑attention, positional encodings, encoder vs decoder vs encoder‑decoder).
  • Embeddings for text, images, audio; encoder–decoder models.
TRN

Training Practices

  • Regularization: dropout, weight decay, early stopping, data augmentation.
  • Learning‑rate schedules, optimizers (SGD, Adam/AdamW), weight initialization.
  • Debugging: vanishing/exploding gradients, overfitting, underfitting.
DOM

1.5 Domain‑Specific AI: CV, NLP, Audio

IOAI Scientific Round and similar contests deliberately cover ML, NLP, and CV.

CV

Computer Vision

  • Image classification with CNNs; transfer learning from pre‑trained encoders.
  • Object detection/segmentation basics (YOLO/SSD/DETR concepts, U‑Net).
  • Data augmentation, dealing with class imbalance, image preprocessing.
NLP

NLP

  • Text preprocessing: tokenization, n‑grams, TF‑IDF.
  • Text classification with classical models and with pre‑trained encoders (BERT‑style).
  • Sequence‑to‑sequence basics (translation, summarization), encoder‑decoder thinking.
AUD

Audio / Multimodal

  • Basics of using pre‑trained speech models for classification or transcription.
  • Converting audio to features (spectrograms, MFCCs) and feeding into CNN/RNN/Transformer.
★

1.6 Contest‑Specific Skills

What separates the top 5% is not just knowledge but contest execution.

  • ›Ability to quickly read a new problem, build a strong baseline within 1–2 hours, then iterate systematically.
  • ›Working under IOAI rules: offline except whitelisted docs, limited libraries, no external APIs, fixed GPU, time‑boxed tasks.
  • ›Strong version control and experiment tracking inside Jupyter (clear notebooks, logging results, commenting decisions).
  • ›Familiarity with past IOAI tasks and best solutions; knowing standard tricks and failure modes.

Phase Roadmap (12‑Month Plan)

This roadmap assumes ~14–18 hours/week. If you can do more, you can compress timelines or go deeper.

0
Weeks 1–2
Phase 0 – Baseline Assessment
Goals
  • Measure your current level in math, Python, and ML.
  • Choose initial difficulty of resources (intro vs intermediate).
Tasks (within first 2 weeks)
  • Take a short diagnostic: solve one simple Kaggle tabular competition end‑to‑end in a weekend.
  • Try to skim and understand at least one IOAI 2024 Scientific Round task (ML or NLP). Don't worry if you can't solve it fully yet.
  • Do a short self‑quiz on math (basic derivatives, matrix multiplication, probability rules).
Deliverable: a one‑page self‑assessment listing strengths, weak areas, and target pace (hours/week).

Objectives
  • Master classical ML algorithms and evaluation.

Weekly Time‑Table Template

Use this as a default; adjust hours to your capacity. Total: 14–18 hours/week. Structure: 5–6 study days + 1 rest day.

Day 1 — Theory3h
  • 1.5 h math (according to current phase).
  • 1.5 h ML/DL theory reading + notes.
Day 2 — Coding3h
  • 2 h implementing algorithms or working on current project.
  • 1 h quick problems (small coding tasks, LeetCode‑style if needed for fluency).
Day 3 — EDA + Models3h
  • 1 h EDA on a new dataset.
  • 2 h build/iterate one or two models.
Day 4 — Contest Sim3h
  • 2–3 h timed session solving a contest‑style task with strict timebox.
Day 5 — Review2–3h
  • Summarize what you learned; clean notebooks.
  • Flashcards or active recall on formulas and key concepts.
Day 6 — Extra / Team≤3h
  • Team practice, IOAI past task re‑runs, or deep dive on a weak area.
Day 7 — Rest—
  • No structured work; at most light reading.
💡 During later phases, convert one of the theory sessions into a full mock contest block (4–5 h) every 2–3 weeks.

Measuring Progress and Adjusting

5.1 Monthly Self‑Check

Every 4 weeks, answer:

  • Can I solve new problems of the same type I studied this month?
  • Did I complete at least 1–2 small projects (not just watching videos)?
  • Do I see improvement in accuracy / leaderboard rank / speed of solving?
If not, slow down and reinforce fundamentals before moving on.

5.2 Contest‑Style Benchmarks

Use these benchmarks (approximate) to judge readiness:

After Phase 2: you should beat baselines on most beginner tabular tasks and understand why.
After Phase 3: you should be able to train a working CNN and a basic fine‑tuned text classifier with decent accuracy on small datasets.
After Phase 4: you should get reasonably close to official IOAI solutions on at least some 2024 tasks when given the same data and time.

5.3 Focus on Weak Links

Top 5% performers don't avoid their weak areas. Each month, pick one weak domain (math topic, CV, NLP, coding speed) and design explicit drills for it.

How to Use This Plan

  1. Print or pin this roadmap and mark phases/weeks on a calendar.
  2. Customize hours/week based on your school and project load.
  3. Log every study session (topic, time, what you produced).
  4. Iterate: if you find IOAI 2024 tasks still too hard after Phase 3, extend that phase or deepen math.

By methodically following these phases, hitting the weekly timetable, and aggressively practising on IOAI‑style tasks and contests, you give yourself a realistic shot at performing in the top 5% against global competitors.
